# App profile and campaign brief

Keep a compact profile in the project's existing marketing directory, or a dedicated `marketing/` directory when appropriate. Follow project instructions for temporary work. Do not modify app source or release metadata merely to create this profile.

## App profile

- **Identity:** app name, project/source, official listing/site, platform, verified release/version and time checked.
- **People and problem:** audience hypothesis, situation that triggers the need, how people handle it today, what becomes easier.
- **Promise:** one clear sentence; avoid a claim broader than the product can deliver.
- **Core loop:** the few actions that produce value; actual control names where relevant.
- **Claims:** claim → supporting current source → limits → suitable screenshot → publication condition. Keep uncertain claims out of finished copy until resolved or qualified.
- **Voice:** a few useful descriptors, sample lines, wording to avoid. Include regulatory or domain-specific claim constraints only where relevant.
- **Visual identity:** fonts and actual files, palette, icon/mark, composition rules, screenshot treatment, approved reference paths/hashes.
- **Distribution:** language, account type/actual handle if known, CTA destination, market/time zone if known. English does not establish a US audience.

Record where facts came from. An old release note can establish what was recorded then, not the current public availability. Refresh only what can affect the new campaign.

## Campaign brief

1. Goal and the audience hypothesis to test.
2. One central campaign idea and a few example hooks.
3. Editorial roles: recognition, usefulness, proof, objections, action; adapt their balance.
4. Calendar: ID, relative/actual day, format, hook, main takeaway, CTA, evidence, and publication condition.
5. Per-post copy and visuals, with all carousel slides or timed Reel scenes.
6. Supporting Stories and any native sticker instructions. Leave usable space for stickers; do not draw fake interactive controls.
7. Account bio, pin order, and Highlights if requested/useful.
8. Learning plan: observation windows, available metrics, comparable formats, and what to try next.

Use post-level metrics only where the platform exposes them. Keep underlying counts beside rates. Leave unavailable data blank. For a small account, repeat a promising idea before treating it as a winner. Do not prescribe a universal posting time or reuse performance thresholds from a different campaign as research-backed facts.

## Continuation state

Record scope, chosen direction, approval evidence if any, unchanged-file hashes, output locations, remaining production, and publication conditions in the campaign itself. Future sessions should be able to continue from those files without rereading the full conversation. Keep this concise; do not create multiple competing status documents.
